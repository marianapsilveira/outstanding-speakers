# Brand Guidelines

## Brand Name

OUTstanding Speakers

---

## Brand Essence

OUTstanding Speakers connects organizations with people willing to share lived experiences.

The goal is not simply to book speakers.

The goal is to create meaningful conversations that increase understanding, empathy and action.

The brand sits at the intersection of:

- human stories
- workplace inclusion
- meaningful dialogue
- lived experience
- social impact

---



## Brand Personality

The brand should feel:

- Human
- Modern
- Intelligent
- Warm
- Honest
- Premium
- Confident

Not performative.

Not overly corporate.

Not activist in a loud or aggressive way.

The impact comes from thoughtful conversations.

---



## Core Brand Themes



### 1. Conversation creates change

Real understanding happens through conversation.

Not through policies alone.

Not through documents alone.

---



### 2. Human stories create empathy

Lived experience creates emotional connection.

Stories are the bridge.

---



### 3. Inclusion happens in practice

Belonging is built in daily interactions.

The workplace is one of the most important environments for that.

---



## Emotional Direction

Users should feel:

- Curious
- Safe
- Inspired
- Open-minded
- Reflective

The experience should create emotional openness without feeling heavy.

---



# Visual Direction



## Overall Feel

The visual identity should feel editorial and premium.

Closer to:

- modern culture publication
- premium product experience
- thoughtful storytelling platform

Less like:

- HR software
- traditional SaaS
- NGO websites

---



## Logo Assets

Primary logo:

- `brand/assets/logo.svg`

The implementation should use the provided logo asset.

Do not recreate the logo with plain text unless explicitly requested.

## Design Language

Strong contrast between:

- dark / immersive sections
- light / clean content sections

Large typography.

Generous spacing.

Strong hierarchy.

---



## Typography

Typography is a major brand element.

Use:

- bold oversized headlines
- clean modern sans-serif
- strong hierarchy

Headlines should feel expressive and memorable.

Examples:

- OUTstanding Speakers
- Meet our Speakers
- Start with a conversation

---



## Color Direction

Primary palette:

- Near-black / deep charcoal
- Warm off-white
- Electric lime accent
- Deep blue-violet gradients
- Muted greys for surfaces

Color should create strong contrast and visual tension.

The palette should feel:

- bold
- modern
- cinematic
- premium
- slightly futuristic

Avoid:

- playful palettes
- pastel-heavy compositions
- bright corporate colors

---



## Motion

Motion should feel subtle and atmospheric.

Not flashy.

Examples:

- slow parallax
- soft hover transitions
- subtle fade-ins
- ambient background movement

Motion should support immersion.

---



## Imagery

Photography should feel:

- real
- intimate
- human
- emotionally grounded

Avoid:

- generic corporate stock images
- overly staged smiles

Portraits should feel authentic and personal.

---



## Backgrounds

Abstract moving backgrounds work well.

Examples:

- soft gradients
- blurred lights
- fluid motion
- subtle video textures

These backgrounds help create emotional atmosphere.

---



# Voice & Tone

The tone should feel:

- thoughtful
- human
- intelligent
- calm
- inviting

Avoid:

- overly corporate language
- buzzwords
- performative DEI language

Write like a thoughtful human, not an HR brochure.

---



# UX Principles



## 1. Reduce friction

The experience should feel easy to navigate.

---



## 2. Build trust

Everything should communicate credibility and care.

---



## 3. Human first

People and stories always come before systems.

---



## 4. Keep emotional clarity

The product should feel emotionally resonant without becoming overwhelming.

---



# Success Criteria

The brand experience succeeds when users feel:

- This feels credible
- This feels modern
- This feels human
- These conversations matter
