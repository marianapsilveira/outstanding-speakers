# Content Strategy

## Purpose

The content strategy defines how the website communicates the value of OUTstanding Speakers and guides users from first understanding the initiative to taking action.

The website should not feel like a generic speaker directory. It should feel like a curated, human-centered platform for meaningful workplace conversations powered by lived experience.

---

## Core Message

Real conversations create meaningful change.

OUTstanding Speakers connects organizations with people willing to share lived experiences in ways that build empathy, reduce bias and support more inclusive workplace cultures.

---



## Strategic Narrative

The website follows a simple narrative arc:

1. Spark attention
2. Explain why conversations matter
3. Build trust through real speakers
4. Support preparation through resources
5. Encourage action through booking or participation

The emotional movement should be:

Curiosity → Understanding → Trust → Relevance → Action

---



## Positioning

OUTstanding Speakers is not positioned as:

- a traditional speaker bureau
- a generic diversity training platform
- a corporate compliance tool
- a passive resource library

It is positioned as:

- a lived-experience speaker network
- a conversation catalyst
- a bridge between personal stories and organizational learning
- a practical way to bring empathy into the workplace

---



## Primary Audiences



### Organizations

Need:

- credible speakers
- clear session formats
- confidence that the experience is professional and safe
- a simple way to request a session

Message:

OUTstanding Speakers helps organizations bring real, human conversations into the workplace with care and structure.

---



### HR teams

Need:

- practical support
- preparation materials
- confidence around sensitivity, consent and psychological safety
- resources before and after the session

Message:

The platform helps HR teams support meaningful conversations without reducing lived experience to a policy exercise.

---



### Employees and managers

Need:

- guidance before participating in conversations
- language and framing
- confidence to ask respectful questions
- ways to continue learning afterwards

Message:

The resources help people prepare with care, listen with openness and keep learning after the conversation.

---



### Speakers

Need:

- clarity about the initiative
- trust that their story will be handled respectfully
- understanding of what participation involves

Message:

OUTstanding Speakers gives people a structured way to share lived experience on their own terms.

---



## Page Roles



### Home

Role:

Introduce the initiative and establish the emotional and strategic value of lived-experience conversations.

The Home page should answer:

- What is this?
- Why does it matter?
- Why should I trust it?
- What can I do next?

Primary actions:

- Explore Speakers
- Book a Speaker

---



### Speakers

Role:

Help users browse, filter and compare speakers.

The Speakers page should answer:

- Who can I invite?
- What topics do they speak about?
- What formats are available?
- Who might fit my organization or audience?

Primary action:

- View Profile

---



### Speaker Profile

Role:

Build trust and help users evaluate speaker fit.

The Speaker Profile page should answer:

- Who is this speaker?
- What is their lived experience or story focus?
- What topics do they speak about?
- What session formats are available?
- What do organizations say about them?

Primary action:

- Request a Session

---



### Resources

Role:

Support people before, during and after conversations.

The Resources page should answer:

- How do we prepare?
- How do we ask better questions?
- How do we support people after the session?
- What should employees, managers and HR each consider?

Primary actions:

- Open Resource
- Browse Speakers
- Book a Speaker

---



### Become a Speaker

Role:

Invite people to participate as speakers and explain what that means.

The Become a Speaker page should answer:

- Who can become a speaker?
- What is expected?
- How is safety handled?
- How does the process work?

Primary action:

- Become a Speaker

---



### Booking Flow

Role:

Convert interest into a structured request without making the experience feel transactional.

The booking flow should feel:

- guided
- respectful
- low-friction
- clear about expectations

It should communicate that sending a request is not an automatic booking. The speaker reviews the context, availability and fit before confirming.

Primary action:

- Send Request

---



## Conversion Strategy

The main conversion path is:

Home → Speakers → Speaker Profile → Booking Flow → Confirmation

Secondary paths:

Home → Resources → Speakers → Booking Flow

Home → Become a Speaker

Speaker Profile → Resources

Resources → Book a Speaker

## Tone of Voice

The tone should be:

- clear
- confident
- human
- calm
- respectful
- contemporary

Avoid:

- overly corporate language
- activist slogans without practical grounding
- infantilizing explanations
- generic DEI clichés
- overly academic language

Preferred language:

- lived experience
- meaningful conversations
- prepare with care
- listen with openness
- keep learning afterwards
- build trust
- reduce bias
- create change through conversation

---



## Content Principles



### 1. Lead with humanity

The website should center people and stories, not policies or frameworks.

### 2. Be precise, not performative

The copy should be intentional and specific. Avoid vague statements about inclusion without explaining how the platform works.

### 3. Make action feel safe

Booking a speaker should feel structured and respectful, not like buying a service from a catalog.

### 4. Balance emotion and credibility

Use emotional storytelling, but anchor it with evidence, preparation and professional structure.

### 5. Respect speaker agency

Speakers are not content assets. The website should communicate that speakers choose how and when to share their experiences.
